Compilation

Finally, when you program on art cluster, you have intel and nvidia compilers, as well as gfortran (GNU Fortran) available. Use the compilers separately (they do not work together very willingly: they have their separate libraries, idiosyncrasies), so normally avoid compiling a part of your project with one and another part by some other compiler. Using different compilers lets you select the one producing best execution speed, as well as to provide a different set of diagnostic messages, which is useful for debugging. The following user/programmer guides are useful, because just saying things like
% compiler_name --help
where compiler_name is either ifort (Intel) [ifx on Rocky], pgif95 (Nvidia) [nvfortran on Rocky OS] or gfortran (GNU project) will spit out some options but not explain them. That's useful for those who've already read compiler docs, so, as they say, RTFM.
□ Intel compiler ifort is documented in ifort-ug+ref.pdf , Intel Fortran Compiler 16.0 User and Reference Guide. Strangely, this different document Intel Fortran Compiler 16.0 User and Reference Guide. is a competing explanation. Both are more than 2000 pages long.
And on the Rocky Linux nodes, a recent version of ifortran, instead of ifort is called ifx.
Being a very good compiler, Intel fortran produces the code for either CPU or MIC (57-core processors called Intel Xeon Phi that we have on cluster nodes called phi-# but unfortunately does not do CUDA Fortran, a language extension supprted by competitor Nividia on their graphics cards only.

Another choice is Nvidia GPU-compatible fortran compiler. On art-1(2) it is invoked as pgf95 (or pgf90). It's a PGI Fortran compiler; Portland Group that first wrote it was later bought by Nvidia.
□ User guide pgi18fortref.pdf is the description of PGI Fortran.

PGI fortran recently was renamed Nvidia Fortran, and is invoked as nvf or nvfortran on Rocky 9 machines. Like ifort, it is also a very good compiler and it is the only one we have implementing CUDA Fortran language extension for GPU computing (unsurprisingly, it can't produce code for MIC).

Compilation

Every C++ compiler should compile a straight C code. We have cc (GNU) C compiler, gcc (GNU c++ compiler). On Rocky systems Nvidia C compiler is invoked as nvc or nvc++. You can also use Intel compilers icc and icpc.

The nvcc compiler does more: it understands CUDA C extensions and will separately compile program's cpu and gpu components, and glue them together in a single executable file at the end (remember that CUDA programs are started on CPU, GPU is a coprocessor).

Aerodynamics of airfoil and one airliner catastrophe

You should get acquainted with the great 100-year old research by a Bavarian physicist Ludwig Prandtl, who created a whole school od aerodynamics, both theoretical and experimental. His students included Theodore von Karman, who became the father of American theoretical aerodynamics & helped U.S. develop first supersonic jets, and Richard von Mises, who achieved many outstanding results in elasticity theory of solids, boundary layer theory and also airfoil theory. Von Mises needs to be mentioned in the context of bad political ideas Prandtl upheld during the Nazi regime in Germany in 1933-1945, which Prandl generally liked and praised (Mises was a Jew from Lemberg = Lwów = L'vov = Lviv).

Page through Prandtl's century-old books Prandtl - Funadamentals of Hydro- and Aeromechanics and Prandtl - Application of modern hydrodynamics to aeronautics
The more modern re-telling of classical works by Prandtl is the subject of Oertel et al. - Prandtl's "Essential Fluid Mechanics" (2004)
The part you are particularly interested in is Lanchester-Prandtl lifting line theory of airfoils.

Pope - Basic Wing and Airfoil Theory (1951) is a great resource.
There are web pages that explain the lifting line calculations.
Katz & Plotkin - Low Speed Aerodynamics (2001) is even better - it's actually all you need to learn about lifting lines/vortices (chapter 8, plus some contents from 9 and 10). You need to generalize to the actual shape of the wing, though, so instead of rectangular vortex with two parts shed and departing to infinity and one part on the straight wing, you need to position N vortices at 1/4 chord behind the leading edge, and N test points at 3/4 chord distance, whatever shape the particular wing has.
Wing shape and other parameters of TU-154M airplane can be figured out from tables and figures in the first chapter in this book (in Russian) .

Your task is to first create a lifting-line theory of a full (test case) and a damaged wing of TU-154 airliner (for which you'll only need the top view of the aircraft and the distance from its axis where a birch tree tore off about 1/3 of the left wingspan, and some infor about the AOA = angle of attack). The theory is formulated as in integral equation, discretized it becomes an NxN linear equations problem, which can be solved with Python. It yields the distribution of stationary lift force along the wing of any shape and asymmetry, so you can derive a torque on a damaged aircraft, needed to predict the precise history of the roll to the left that develops, wheter or not the aircraft was controllable by pilots at this stage and such.

Next, given sufficient time, you may want to generalize the method to full panel method in which vortex lines are layed out not in the form of lines like in Prandtl's scheme, but around N polygonal panels covering the upper and lower surface of the wing. I haven't gone so far in my calculations, we'll see if the panel theory gives similar results.

Finally, learn about SPH method (below) and use it for full 3-D fluid dynamics, if you still have time left! It's an open-ended project, like most..
