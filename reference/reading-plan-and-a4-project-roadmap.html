1. Pre-Midterm 7-Week Reading Curriculum

This curriculum structures your weekly reading for the first half of PHYD57, aligning lecture concepts, homework deliverables (A1 and A2), and the Midterm examination.

PHYD57 First Half Architecture:
├── Weeks 1–3: Systems Foundations, Linux, Compilers & Languages (C vs. Fortran vs. Python)
├── Weeks 3–4: Assignment 1 (A1) Mastery — Flight Loops, Ballistics, Monotonicity & Stencils
├── Weeks 5–6: Assignment 2 (A2) Mastery — Monte Carlo Integration, OpenMP & 2D Diffusion
└── Week 7:    Midterm Exam — Physical Bottlenecks, Quiz Terminology & Written Problems

Week 1 (Lecture 1): Course Scope and Foundations of Scientific Computing

  • Lecture Focus: Course mechanics, computing in physics history, language taxonomy.
  • Norbert Schörghofer — Lessons in Scientific Computing:
    • Chapter 1 (§1.1–§1.4, pp. 1–35): Read the language comparison (C, Fortran 95, Python, NumPy, MATLAB, IDL). Understand why interpreted dynamic typing incurs massive execution overhead in loop-heavy scientific simulations.
  • Paul E. Ceruzzi — A History of Modern Computing:
    • Chapter 5: Early computer architectures, transition from mainframes to microprocessors, and numerical origins in post-war physics.

Week 2 (Lecture 2): Microprocessors, Linux Essentials and Terminal Environments

  • Lecture Focus: Microprocessor evolution, CentOS environment, bash vs. tcsh shells, terminal text editors (vi, nano, micro).
  • Scott Membrey — The Definitive Guide to CentOS:
    • Chapter 3: Shell navigation, process management (ps, bg, fg, kill, &), stream redirection (>, >>, |), and remote administration.
    • Shell divergence: Note how environment variables and aliases differ between bash (export VAR=val, alias name='cmd') and tcsh on art-1 (setenv VAR val, alias name 'cmd').
  • Text Editor Fluency:
    • Command mode, insert mode, and visual mode in vi/vim (saving :wq, search /pattern, replace :%s/old/new/g), alongside basic editing in nano and micro.

Week 3 (Lecture 3): Connectivity, Compilers and The 2D Diffusion Benchmark

  • Lecture Focus: ssh/sftp, network security (/etc/hosts.deny), GNU/Intel/NVIDIA compilers, and benchmarking the 2D Laplace operator stencil.
  • Language Foundations (Select C or Modern Fortran):
    • For Modern Fortran: J. Izaac & J. B. Wang — Computational Quantum Mechanics, Chapter 2 (pp. 35–75): Modules, strict typing (implicit none), array slices (A(1:N:2)), dynamic allocation (allocate/deallocate), and column-major contiguous memory.
    • For C: K. N. King — C Programming: A Modern Approach, Chapters 11, 12, & 17: Pointer arithmetic, heap allocation (malloc/free), row-major order, and 1D flattened contiguous buffers (grid[i * N + j]) vs. cache-thrashing jagged arrays (grid[i][j]).
  • Randall J. LeVeque — Finite Difference Methods for ODEs and PDEs:
    • Chapter 9: 2D 5-point Laplace stencil discretization for the diffusion equation.
  • Compiler Manuals & Optimization Flags:
    • GNU (gcc, gfortran), Intel oneAPI (icx, ifx), and NVIDIA HPC SDK (nvcc, nvfortran). Understand -O2, -O3, -march=native, and -fopenmp.

Week 4 (Lecture 4): C/Fortran Deep Dive and Assignment 1 (A1) Hand-In

  • Lecture Focus: Algorithmic puzzles (the 711 puzzle, Kruskal counts & linked lists), Python-C bindings, and submitting Assignment 1.
  • Problem 1.1 (F-16 Aerobatic Loop):
    • John D. Anderson — Introduction to Flight, Chapter 5 (§5.13–§5.16): Accelerated flight, load factor \(n = 1 + v^2/(gR)\), centripetal pull-up force balance, apex weightlessness (\(R = v_0^2/(5g)\)), and dynamic stall envelopes (\(v_S(n) = v_{S,0}\sqrt{n}\)).
    • William H. Press et al. — Numerical Recipes (3rd Ed.), Chapter 16 (§16.1–§16.3): Runge–Kutta 4th-order (RK4) ODE integration.
  • Problem 1.2 (Air-Water Multiphase Ballistics):
    • John R. Taylor — Classical Mechanics, Chapter 2 (§2.1–§2.4): Quadratic aerodynamic drag (\(\mathbf{F}_D = -\frac{1}{2}\rho C_D A v\mathbf{v}\)) and closed-form vertical terminal velocity.
    • RHK — Physics, Vol. 1, Chapter 15 (§15.1–§15.4): Archimedes’ principle and effective underwater gravity \(g_{\text{eff}} = g(1 - \rho_w/\rho_p)\).
  • Problem 1.3 (Vector Monotonicity and Running Scans):
    • CLRS — Introduction to Algorithms, Chapters 3 & 4: Asymptotic proof that naive pairwise comparisons scale as \(\Theta(N^2)\), reducible to a single-pass \(\Theta(N)\) running-minimum scan with \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) storage.
    • Bryant & O’Hallaron (CS:APP), Chapters 5 & 6: Hardware cache lines (64-byte chunks), spatial/temporal locality, and eliminating strided memory stalls.
  • Problem 1.4 (Second-Derivative Stencil Limits):
    • Mark Newman — Computational Physics, Chapter 5 (§5.10): Subtractive floating-point cancellation (\(E_{\text{round}} = 4\epsilon_{\text{mach}}|f| / h^2\)), derivation of optimal step size \(h_{\text{opt}} \sim \epsilon_{\text{mach}}^{1/4}\), and plotting the log-log error V-curve.
  • In-Class Puzzles:
    • 711 Puzzle: Prime factorization \(711 \times 10^6 = 2^6 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^6 \cdot 79\). Constraint pruning (isolating multiples of 79, ordering \(a \le b \le c \le d\)) to turn an \(\mathcal{O}(N^4)\) search into an instant millisecond loop.
    • Kruskal Counts: Pointer/index hopping across card sequences mapping to linked lists.

Week 5 (Lecture 5): Monte Carlo Methods and Shared-Memory Parallelism (OpenMP)

  • Lecture Focus: Pseudo-random numbers (finding \(\pi\)), statistical error scaling (\(\sigma \propto 1/\sqrt{N}\)), and OpenMP fork-join multi-threading.
  • Mark Newman — Computational Physics:
    • Chapter 10 (§10.1–§10.3): Linear congruential generators (LCGs), random seeds, hit-or-miss integration, and Central Limit Theorem proof of dimension-independent \(\sigma \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\) convergence.
  • Press et al. — Numerical Recipes:
    • Chapter 7 (§7.1–§7.3): Uniform random deviates and avoiding hyper-plane lattice artifacts (Marsaglia effect).
    • Chapter 15 (§15.1): Sample variance and confidence interval modeling.
  • Georg Hager & Gerhard Wellein — Introduction to High Performance Computing:
    • Chapter 6 (§6.1–§6.4): OpenMP fork-join model, #pragma omp parallel for (or !$omp parallel do), thread safety, data scoping (shared vs. private), and reduction(+:sum) clauses to prevent mutex lock contention.

Week 6 (Lecture 6): Physical Hardware Limits, 2D OpenMP Diffusion and A2 Hand-In

  • Lecture Focus: Single-thread performance walls, Dennard scaling breakdown, parallelizing the 2D heat equation, submitting Assignment 2, and finalizing A4 project groups.
  • Hager & Wellein — Introduction to High Performance Computing:
    • Chapter 1: Pipelining, superscalar execution, and SIMD vector registers.
    • Chapter 2 (§2.1–§2.3): Arithmetic intensity (FLOPs per byte of DRAM transfer) and the memory bandwidth wall.
    • Chapter 3: Breakdown of Dennard scaling (leakage currents and the thermal “power wall” around 2004–2005) making multi-core parallelism the only path forward.
  • Randall J. LeVeque — Finite Difference Methods:
    • Chapter 9: Parallelizing explicit 2D 5-point Laplace grid updates under OpenMP, handling boundary conditions, and verifying the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) stability bound (\(\Delta t \le \Delta x^2 / (4D)\)).

Week 7 (Lecture 7): Midterm Exam Mastery (In-Class 11:10–12:10)

  • Exam Structure: 16% total mark (8% Written Problems + 8% Multiple-Choice/Quiz). Allowed aid: up to 8 handwritten pages (4 double-sided sheets) and a calculator.
  • Quiz Terminology Review:
    • Review historical and hardware concepts: bit vs. Byte, ASCII, bitrate, data bus, L1/L2/L3 cache latency vs. DRAM bandwidth, Moore’s Law vs. Dennard scaling, and /etc/hosts.deny.
  • Instructor Portal and Code Archives:
    • Review mid-prep-2026.html and the 2025 Midterm Exam solutions. Watch out for quiz traps where incorrect technical assertions are placed inside quadruple asterisks (****word****).
    • Re-run and trace sample codes from progD57 (the 711 solver, Kruskal counts, OpenMP Monte Carlo \(\pi\), and parallel 2D diffusion).

2. Assignment 4 (A4) Group Research Project Menu

The group research project (23% of course grade, groups finalized in Lecture 6, presentations in Lecture 12) requires building an end-to-end HPC simulation. Below are the recommended project directions curated by Prof. Artymowicz.

A4 Group Project Focus Areas:
├── Track A: Astrophysical & Geophysical Fluid Dynamics (SPH & Disks)
├── Track B: High-Speed Aerodynamics & Flight Reconstruction (Smolensk Tu-154)
├── Track C: Massively Parallel Gravitational & Orbital Dynamics (FFT N-Body & TRAPPIST-1)
├── Track D: Structural Mechanics & Resonance (Golden Gate Bridge)
└── Track E: Econophysics & Machine Learning (Algorithmic Trading from Noisy Signals)

Track A: Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) Fluid Simulation

  • Physical System: Implement Lagrangian particle-based hydrodynamics without a fixed grid.
  • Project Options: Protoplanetary disk gas dynamics with a giant planet exciting spiral density waves; gas accretion streams feeding a close binary star system; or supersonic turbulent gas jet propagating into ambient medium.
  • Computational Challenge: Implementing smoothing kernels (cubic spline \(W(r, h)\)). Accelerating \(\mathcal{O}(N^2)\) neighbor particle searches using a linked-list grid cell index (Cell-Linked Lists) to achieve \(\mathcal{O}(N)\) scaling on multi-core CPU (OpenMP) or GPU (CUDA).
  • Key Literature: J. J. Monaghan — Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (1997, 2005); Stephan Rosswog — Astrophysical Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics.

Track B: Aerodynamics of Damaged Flight (Smolensk Tu-154 Reconstruction)

  • Physical System: Numerical aerodynamic modeling of what happens to a large passenger jet when it loses a portion of its wing (such as the Tu-154M crash at Smolensk).
  • Project Scope: Model 3D lifting surfaces using the Vortex Lattice Method (VLM) or 3D panel methods. Calculate spanwise lift distribution, asymmetrical rolling moments, and induced drag following partial wing loss. Simulate dynamic flight-path divergence (uncontrollable roll vs. aileron/rudder deflection limits).
  • Key Literature: Ludwig Prandtl — Fundamentals of Hydro- and Aeromechanics; John D. Anderson — Fundamentals of Aerodynamics; Prof. Artymowicz’s aerodynamic flight analysis portal at planets.utsc.utoronto.ca/~pawel/s/.

Track C: GPU-Accelerated N-Body Collapse via Particle-Mesh FFT

  • Physical System: Gravitational collapse of a dense galactic cluster containing central supermassive black holes.
  • Project Scope: Direct all-pairs summation scales as \(\mathcal{O}(N^2)\), which limits simulations to small \(N\). Implement a Particle-Mesh (PM) gravity solver: assign particle mass to a 3D grid (Cloud-in-Cell interpolation), solve the Poisson equation \(\nabla^2 \Phi = 4\pi G \rho\) in Fourier space using Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT), differentiate \(\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Phi\), and interpolate forces back to particles in \(\mathcal{O}(N \ln N)\) time.
  • GPU Acceleration: Implement the 3D FFT on GPU using NVIDIA’s CUFFT library.
  • Key Literature: James Binney & Scott Tremaine — Galactic Dynamics; E. Oran Brigham — The Fast Fourier Transform and Its Applications; NVIDIA CUFFT Library Documentation.

Track D: Structural Mechanics and Dynamics of the Golden Gate Bridge

  • Physical System: Dynamic finite-element / lumped-parameter elasticity model of a suspension bridge under turbulent wind excitation and vehicle traffic.
  • Project Scope: Discretize bridge deck, towers, main cables, and vertical suspenders into coupled spring-mass and beam elements. Formulate equations of motion under vortex-induced shedding and aerodynamic gust forces. Identify eigenmodes: distinguish vertical bending oscillations from catastrophic torsional flutter (the Tacoma Narrows resonance phenomenon).
  • Key Literature: N. J. Gimsing — Cable Supported Bridges: Concept and Design; M. Gazzola et al. — Mathematical Models for Suspension Bridges; Press et al. — Numerical Recipes, Chapter 11 (Eigensystems).

Track E: Econophysics and Machine Learning from Ultra-Noisy Signals

  • Physical System: Modeling financial time series, market microstructure, and algorithmic risk using neural networks and stochastic differential equations (SDEs).
  • Project Scope: High-frequency limit order book simulation or predicting volatility transitions from noisy tick data. Implement Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM/GRU) or 1D Convolutional Neural Networks in C++/CUDA or PyTorch. Analyze overfitting, hyperparameter regularization, and the Sharpe ratio under transaction friction.
  • Key Literature: Taylor & Shadbolt — Neural Networks and Financial Markets; Charu C. Aggarwal — Neural Networks and Deep Learning; Marcos López de Prado — Advances in Financial Machine Learning.

Track F: Orbital Dynamics and Resonance in the TRAPPIST-1 System

  • Physical System: Long-term orbital resonance and gravitational stability of the 7-planet TRAPPIST-1 system.
  • Project Scope: Simulate the 3-body Laplace-type resonances and resonant chains (\(8:5, 5:3, 3:2, 3:2, 4:3, 3:2\)). Build a symplectic orbital integrator (Wisdom–Holman map or 4th-order symplectic Runge–Kutta) to conserve angular momentum and energy across millions of planetary orbits without numerical dissipation.
  • Key Literature: Carl D. Murray & Stanley F. Dermott — Solar System Dynamics; Press et al. — Numerical Recipes, Chapter 17 (Symplectic Integrators).

### Track F: Orbital Dynamics and Resonance in the TRAPPIST-1 System

Build a symplectic orbital integrator (Wisdom–Holman map or 4th-order symplectic Runge–Kutta) to conserve angular momentum and energy across millions of planetary orbits without numerical dissipation.
* **Key Literature:** Carl D. Murray & Stanley F. Dermott — *Solar System Dynamics*; Press et al. — *Numerical Recipes*, Chapter 17 (Symplectic Integrators).
